## **1. What is this sprint actually asking you to do?**

In simple terms:

You have three days to identify a problem related to how AI understands, communicates, evaluates, or uses information.

Then you:

1. Choose a research question.
2. Investigate the problem.
3. Build a small prototype, benchmark, tool, or research study.
4. Test your idea.
5. Write a report explaining your findings.

You can participate individually or in a team of up to five people.

You don't necessarily need to build an AI model. Some projects require only research, careful analysis, and structured evaluation.

## **3. Three project ideas I would seriously consider**

These are potential projects that can be scoped to three days.

### **Idea 1: Does AI actually help us make better decisions?**

My favourite for you to explore

**Research question:** Does AI provide advice that genuinely improves people's decisions, or does it simply make people feel more confident?

You could:

- Create realistic scenarios involving career choices, education, or relationships.
- Ask AI models to provide advice.
- Evaluate their recommendations against a predefined decision-quality rubric.
- Examine whether the advice considers uncertainty, alternatives, risks, and long-term consequences.

**Why it matters:** An answer can sound intelligent and persuasive while being harmful to someone's decision-making.

### **Idea 2: Can AI admit when it doesn't know something?**

**Research question:** How accurately can AI recognize the limits of its own knowledge?

You could:

- Prepare questions with different levels of difficulty.
- Ask AI models to answer and report their confidence.
- Include questions with insufficient information.
- Measure how frequently they confidently produce incorrect answers instead of acknowledging uncertainty.

This would fit Track 1.

### **Idea 3: Does AI make disagreements worse?**

**Research question:** When people discuss conflicts with AI, does it encourage constructive solutions or reinforce their existing beliefs?

You could:

- Prepare realistic interpersonal conflict scenarios.
- Give different perspectives of the same disagreement to an AI.
- Evaluate whether its advice encourages understanding, compromise, and de-escalation.
- Develop a scoring rubric for constructive advice.

This would fit Track 3.

## **4. What would make a project competitive?**

The most important thing is not building something complicated.

A strong project should have:

| Element        | What judges should see                    |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Clear question | A specific problem worth investigating    |
| Methodology    | A reproducible way to investigate it      |
| Evidence       | Actual experiments or collected data      |
| Findings       | Results, including unexpected ones        |
| Impact         | Why the findings matter in the real world |

A small experiment with convincing evidence can be more valuable than an impressive-looking prototype without meaningful evaluation.
